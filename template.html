Yes, incidents are more frequent: every like-for-like measure found is up, from about half again (ransomware, GitHub) to more than double (network outage events).

Caveats: ThousandEyes's monitoring coverage grows over time. Leak-site counts include unverified claims and miss victims who paid. Reporting and Downdetector use rise after big outages. ThousandEyes counts for Nov 2–15, 2026 were below the Aug–Sep 2026 peaks, so the November cluster was not a jump in the base rate.

The November spike (Nov 12–17)

  • Nov 12: Microsoft 365 global outage, about 24h. Cause: a latent July gateway bug triggered by Teams failover load (error).
  • Nov 13: FAA nationwide ground stop, about 12,462 cancellations. Router work by contractor L3Harris flooded the FAA network (maintenance/upgrade). Workday's HR platform also went down.
  • Nov 15: AWS us-east-1 failures for 3h (sign-up, IAM Identity Center, load balancers). No root cause published.
  • Nov 16: Chase software bug and a United IT outage, both non-malicious. Optimizely and Infoblox were real attacks.
  • Nov 17: NSANet ("routine maintenance") and Apple ("internal update") went down, plus the National Weather Service, Marriott and a Salesforce outage on three US instances.

Fourteen of the %%N%% incidents, including two of the three severity-5 events, landed in six days. Officials and researchers see no sign of coordination: DHS, NBC's sources, SentinelOne, Unit 42 and academics at Notre Dame and Virginia Tech all point to routine changes in highly concentrated, tightly coupled systems. Lawmakers have asked for briefings because several explanations are still one-liners.

Method and limits

  • Window: Aug 17 – Nov 17, 2026. Sources: vendor status pages and post-incident reviews (Azure, Google Cloud, GitHub, Salesforce, Cloudflare, NATS, FAA, Revolut), major outlets (BBC, Guardian, Reuters, AP, CNBC, BleepingComputer, The Record, TechCrunch, SecurityWeek), CM-Alliance and SOCRadar monthly roundups, ThousandEyes weekly reports, and X news summaries as a cross-check.
  • Cause labels follow the officially stated cause. Attack: a malicious actor confirmed or credibly claimed. Spoof-Error: config error, bad deploy, software defect, human error, hardware or power fault, or false reports. Maintenance: the official cause is planned maintenance or upgrade work. Unknown: no cause published or confirmed, never a default to attack.
  • Severity is an editorial 1–5 judgement: users affected, duration, money, data exposed. Five means catastrophic or global.
  • This is a curated list of notable, citable incidents. Thousands of minor status-page blips and individual ransomware leak-site claims are counted only in aggregate, in the year-over-year section.
  • Every source link was checked and none returned 404. Some outlets (AP, Reuters, NSA, Revolut and a few local sites) block automated clients but load in a browser.
  • Raw data: data.json.
Last updated Nov 17, 2026. Causes are as officially stated by the affected organisations or authorities at the time of writing and may change as investigations conclude. Not affiliated with any organisation listed.
